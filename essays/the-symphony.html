The Symphony

Ark Saini · August 2026

There is a strange urge some people carry: the desire to learn everything. Theology, history, philosophy, design, writing, music, science. Not one of them but all of them, the way you want the whole sky and not just a window's worth.

I carry it too.

For years it felt like a flaw. Pick a niche, the world kept saying, and the world had a point. I would have loved to report that I listened. This essay, which touches seven disciplines in its first sentence, is my confession that I did not. People kept offering me the cure: specialize. I would agree enthusiastically, resolve to finally pick my one thing, and then be found three days later reading about Byzantine history for no reason I could defend.

But all that undisciplined wandering taught me something: knowledge isn't actually divided.

Listen closely and the disciplines stop sounding like separate rooms. They start sounding like a symphony. Each one is an instrument, tuned to its own register, playing the same score.

Theology carries the deepest line. Why is there anything? What is good? What waits beyond the visible? Philosophy holds the structure, asking what truth even is and how creatures like us could ever reach it. History is the orchestra's memory, every theme humanity has attempted, every crescendo and collapse, kept so we don't repeat them blindly. Art, design and music are perception itself made audible, how reality feels from inside a human life. And science and mathematics play the pattern underneath everything. Matter, motion, energy, time. The hidden lattice of the world.

Heard separately, they are subjects. Heard together, they are one piece of music. And once you hear it, you cannot unhear it.

The minds I admire most heard it too. Newton wrote more pages on theology than on physics. To him, gravity and God were one investigation. Ramanujan said an equation had no meaning for him unless it expressed a thought of God. Tesla chased the universe through frequency and vibration, physics sliding into music. Da Vinci refused the border entirely. His paintings ran on anatomy, optics and mathematics, and he saw no contradiction in that. Analysis and creativity were one curiosity wearing two coats.

But this hunger has its own dilemma, and I feel it daily. Curiosity wants every field at once. Depth demands you stay in one place long enough to grow roots. Follow only curiosity and you become a collector of surfaces. Follow only depth and the symphony narrows to a single note. The thinkers I admire resolved it the same way: they chose one instrument, and let the whole orchestra teach them how to play it.

I am choosing philosophy. From that center I want to reach history, psychology, art, music and science, not as separate worlds but as reflections of the same one. My task is not to know everything at once. It is to build from one sacred center and let all knowledge return to it, like tributaries to a river.

A brief introduction, then, since this is my first letter to you.

I'm Ark. I just turned 25, and I've promised myself I'll write every day. Even one line. Even a phrase. It counts.

My mother is a professor of English literature. She submitted her PhD thesis, gave birth to me, then went back and defended it. I learned early that a woman can carry two things at once. That's the house I was born into. Language first, everything else after. I don't remember a time before books. There wasn't one. Her words and thoughts and I grew up together, under shelves of literature from across the world, texts not written only in English, many carried into it by translation. History, philosophy, human thought pressed into poems, stories, dramas. A house loud with books and quiet with people. As a kid, when a conversation was too hard to have, I didn't have it. I wrote a letter instead. Slid under a door for my parents, tucked inside a book for my friends. Paper was where I was fluent. It still is.

This year the feelings got too big to store, so I did the old thing. I wrote. They became poems. Then one evening I read one aloud to my friends. All my life I had handed people my words and left before they finished reading. This time I stayed. I watched their faces while my lines landed. I liked it, and I still don't know what surprised me more, the liking or the staying.

So this space will hold philosophy, mostly. Self-taught, since the degree has to wait for a while. And the rest of the pieces of the puzzle I'm trying to put together: living without a phone in a glowing world, looking for a career that fits, moving through the world neurodivergent (my own take on being "quirky" and weird), building a self worth the trouble. I won't promise a lane. Lanes are the thing I'm arguing against.

And how do I even introduce who I am? A friend? A daughter? Do my degrees define me, or my work, or my kindness? Or how I look? How I make you feel? How I make you think? A stranger who smiles at you? A random person whose thoughts you happen to be reading? I don't know. But here I am, trying to make sense of it.

One line a day. Some of them will land here, slid under your door.

Bear with me. Stay a little longer if you like. Or fold this one twice and keep it in your pocket.
